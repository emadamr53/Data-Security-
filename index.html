Foundation

The CIA triad

Every security program balances three core properties. When one fails, the whole system is at risk.

🔒

Confidentiality

Only authorized people can read sensitive data. Encryption, access controls, and classification labels enforce who sees what.

  • Encryption
  • RBAC
  • NDA
✓

Integrity

Data stays accurate and unaltered. Hashing, digital signatures, and audit logs detect tampering before damage spreads.

  • Checksums
  • Signing
  • Audits
⚡

Availability

Systems and data are reachable when needed. Redundancy, DDoS mitigation, and disaster recovery keep services online.

  • Backups
  • HA clusters
  • SLA
Landscape

Threats you should know

Attackers exploit technology and psychology. Recognizing patterns is the first step to stopping them.

High

Phishing & social engineering

Fake emails and messages trick users into revealing credentials or installing malware.

Critical

Ransomware

Encrypts files and demands payment. Regular offline backups are your strongest defense.

Medium

Insider risk

Employees or partners with excessive access can leak or misuse data—intentionally or by mistake.

High

Unpatched software

Known vulnerabilities in OS and apps are easy targets. Patch management closes the gap.

Action

Security best practices

Small habits compound into strong protection—for individuals and teams alike.

  1. 01

    Use strong, unique passwords

    A password manager generates and stores credentials so you never reuse passwords across sites.

  2. 02

    Enable multi-factor authentication

    MFA adds a second layer—even if a password leaks, attackers still cannot sign in.

  3. 03

    Encrypt data at rest and in transit

    TLS protects data in motion; full-disk and database encryption protect stored information.

  4. 04

    Follow least privilege

    Grant only the access each role needs. Review permissions regularly and revoke what is unused.
